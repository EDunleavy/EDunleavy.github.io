A Place for What I Build and Where Life Takes Me

With graduation getting closer, I wanted a place where I could introduce myself and share the things I spend my time working on. I’m studying finance at the University of Oklahoma, and as I think about life after college, I’ve been thinking about how people get to know me—especially people I haven’t had the chance to meet yet.

A resume gives someone a useful overview of my education, experience, and skills. There’s also a lot about me that takes more room to explain: what catches my attention, why I decide to build something, what I do outside of school, and how I approach a problem when I don’t already know the answer.

That’s why I made this website. I wanted a more personal, technology-forward way to share my work and give people a deeper introduction to who I am. It gives me room to talk about a project whether it relates directly to finance or started because I thought, “That would be cool. I wonder if I could make it.”

I also wanted somewhere to keep track of my life as it happens. Projects, travel, important experiences, and whatever I learn along the way all have a place here.

Curiosity Has Always Been Part of It

I’ve been interested in how things work for as long as I can remember. One memory that sticks with me is a laptop my dad had that broke beyond repair. When he was getting rid of it, I asked if I could have it so I could take it apart. I must have been about thirteen or fourteen.

I was curious about what was inside. I’d used laptops, but I wanted to see the pieces that made one work and how they all fit together.

My interest in figuring things out started before that, but that laptop probably helped establish my habit of disassembling things. Occasionally, I got a little ahead of myself and took something apart before it was actually broken or needed any attention.

That curiosity still influences what I do today. Sometimes it turns into a useful tool. Sometimes it leads to a design I want to try 3D printing. Other times, I spend a while learning about something simply because I want to understand it. I enjoy being able to follow a question far enough to try something for myself.

This website gives me a place to share where those questions lead.

Finance, Building, and Having Different Interests

The first project I want to introduce here is my Excel loan payment and extra-payment calculator. It has the clearest connection to what I study, and it’s a good example of using something I’m learning to build a practical tool. I’ll get into how it works and what I want to do with it in a separate post.

Other projects will cover different interests, including a flight radar I built for my brother, a desktop time and weather widget, and things I design and 3D print.

I like having that variety. Studying finance influences how I think about costs, resources, and what it takes to get an idea off the ground. Even a small personal project involves decisions about what you need, what you can afford, and where it makes sense to spend your time or money.

I also enjoy having hobbies simply because I like doing them. Some of my projects will connect closely to school or a future career. Others will come from a personal interest or an everyday problem I feel like solving.

Having room for school, work, and a personal life matters to me. I want this website to reflect that range of interests because they’re all part of how I spend my time and who I am.

Building the Website Was Its Own Project

One of the biggest surprises in this process was how approachable creating a website turned out to be.

I started with very little experience in this area. With help and direction from AI, I had something online that I was extremely happy with in a matter of days. ChatGPT and Codex helped guide me through the steps, explain unfamiliar parts of the process, and work toward the result I had in mind.

The best way I can describe their role is that they were like a manager guiding me through a project in a field I hadn’t worked in before. I could explain what I wanted the website to do and how I wanted it to feel, then get help figuring out the next step.

I brought my ideas, interests, personal information, and opinions about what felt right. AI helped turn those into a working website and gave me direction when I didn’t know how to move forward.

That experience is worth sharing openly, especially because I hope other people see this and feel encouraged to try something themselves. You can start learning while you’re making something. There are resources available to help you work through the unfamiliar parts, and asking questions can take you a long way.

The website will keep changing as I come up with additions and learn more about what I can do with it. I’m happy with where it is now, and I’m looking forward to seeing how it develops alongside my knowledge and experience.

Sharing the Process

As I add projects, I want to explain enough that someone else could understand what I made and have a reasonable starting point for making their own version.

Depending on the project, that might include a materials list, a brief explanation of why it works, pictures showing the main parts, and any relevant code or Excel files. I want someone who finds an idea interesting to be able to explore it further without having to guess at every step.

I also want to document setbacks. Making something involves decisions, attempts that don’t work, changes in direction, and details that turn out to matter more than expected. Those are useful things to share. If I run into a problem and eventually figure it out, that experience might save someone else time—or at least help them understand why their first attempt isn’t working.

The projects here will vary in size and complexity. Some may be small tools that solve a specific problem. Others may grow as I think of new things to add. I want to give myself room to share both.

Part of the purpose of keeping this record is being able to look back and see how an idea developed, what I learned, and what I would approach differently the next time.

Life Beyond the Projects

I want this blog to include personal experiences, too. I hope to write about travel, important events in my life, and things that give me a new perspective.

As graduation approaches, there’s a lot I’m still figuring out. I don’t have a specific company or city picked out for what comes next. I hope to find a company I enjoy being part of, in a city where I can settle down, expand my interests, and meet like-minded people.

That’s a broad goal, but it’s an honest description of where I am right now. I’m looking forward to learning more about the kind of work I enjoy and the kind of life I want to build around it.

Keeping a website through that transition feels worthwhile. It gives me somewhere to document the experiences and interests that develop along the way, and somewhere to return to when I want to see how much has changed.

If You’ve Been Thinking About Making Something

One thing I hope someone takes away from this website is that there are plenty of ways to get help when you want to create something.

You can ask someone who works in the field, find a guide, look for examples, or use AI to help you understand where to start. AI has made it easier to access information and ask follow-up questions when an explanation doesn’t make sense yet.

You can end up with so much information that your head hurts a little. I think that’s part of the fun, even if it doesn’t always feel that way in the moment.

My advice is to jump in and ask the first question. Pick something you’re interested in and start learning what it would take to make it happen. There may be more involved than you expected, and you may need to adjust your idea as you learn, but giving yourself the chance to try is worth it.

Being able to have an idea and turn it into something real is an amazing feeling. I hope sharing my own projects encourages someone else to experience that for themselves.

Take a Look Around

This website is for me, and it’s also for anyone who wants to learn more about me or has an interest in something I’m building.

If you’re a potential employer, I hope it gives you a fuller picture of my personality, curiosity, and abilities. If you’re here because of a project, I hope you find something useful—or something you’d like to try making yourself.

Feel free to explore my projects, read future blog posts, or take a look at my resume. If you have a question, an idea for improving a project or the website, or an opportunity you think could be a good fit, you can reach me at eric_dunleavy@yahoo.com.

I enjoy meeting people, hearing how they approach things, and gaining a different perspective.

I’m a normal guy who enjoys learning and making things, and I’m glad to have a place to share that. Thanks for taking a look.

— Eric
